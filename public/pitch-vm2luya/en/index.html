Privacy and trust design: who can see the documents

Encrypted by default

Profile answers and uploads are encrypted; only the applicant and the processing software open them.

Data minimization

De-identify context before AI calls; send only what the task needs.

Source transparency

Each requirement links to its official source with a last-checked date.

Material labeling

Official forms, official guidance, product interpretation and AI templates are labeled apart.

User control

Users can inspect, export and delete their workspace.

No approval promised

Visa decisions stay with governments.

No-human-access and de-identification are architecture goals, not an audited guarantee; we do not claim any system is risk-free.

BACKUP V10 · VISA MASTERIf asked: AI hallucinates. How do you keep visa information accurate?

AI accuracy: how multi-layer verification works

1 · Official sources as ground truth

Consulate and visa-center requirements, with source and last-checked date, by consular district.

2 · Multi-path cross-checking

Several independent paths do the same task and are compared. Agreement raises confidence, but agreement is not proof.

3 · Structured validation

Three checks: official rules, field consistency, completeness.

4 · Freshness and versioning

Rules are versioned; stale or conflicting sources are flagged for review.

5 · Escalate when unsure

On disagreement or low confidence, the item is not presented as fact; it is escalated.

6 · Model-agnostic

Better foundation models make research and form-filling cheaper and more accurate; the verification layer stays.

Governments decide visas; we never promise approval.

BACKUP V12 · VISA MASTERIf asked: is this only your family's problem?

What other applicants report: third-party evidence

Appointment slots are sold

Fliggy listings offered Spain-visa slots for ¥268–¥418 with hundreds of monthly sales; BLS warns against third-party slot buying. [1, 2]

Days of preparation, months of waiting

A Shanghai-district applicant refreshed BLS for two weeks; a first-time U.S. applicant spent 2–3 days on DS-160; B1/B2 interview waits ran about two months in September 2026. [3, 4]

Official pages can conflict

BLS Chengdu notices describe both walk-in access and appointment requirements; the EU Delegation ran a campaign on common Schengen mistakes in March 2025. [1, 2]

Refusal and refund limits

About 4% Schengen refusal for applications lodged in China (2025) versus a 20.2% adjusted U.S. B-visa refusal rate (FY2025). A ¥698 'full refund if refused' listing refunded only its own fee. [2, 4]

Collected September 2026; personal accounts are anecdotes, and the two refusal measures are not directly comparable.
